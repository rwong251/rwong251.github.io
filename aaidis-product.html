AAIDIS · Strategic Decision Intelligence

When no single analysis is enough, evaluate the decision as a whole.

AAIDIS is designed for consequential strategic choices where several uncertain forces, competing objectives, external reactions, and asymmetric consequences must be considered together.

The AAIDIS role
Bring the evidence, scenarios, trade-offs, and alternatives into the decision itself.

AAIDIS sits above isolated analyses and asks the executive question: given everything relevant, what should we do?

Strategic situations

Decisions where the cost of a weak choice can compound.

Should we acquire this company—or walk away?

The answer may depend on valuation, financing, market outlook, synergies, integration risk, competitive response, downside scenarios, and the value of waiting.

Decision value: evaluate the acquisition as an integrated strategic choice rather than a collection of separate analyses.
Should we enter this market now, later, or not at all?

Demand, competitor reaction, regulation, investment requirements, learning, reversibility, and timing can all change the answer.

Decision value: compare entry paths and the cost of committing too early, too late, or not at all.
Should we expand capacity while demand is uncertain?

A forecast is only one input. The decision also depends on capital at risk, competitor expansion, operating flexibility, option value, and what happens if the forecast is wrong.

Decision value: compare strategic alternatives across plausible futures rather than one expected case.
When should we abandon or pivot an existing strategy?

Management needs to distinguish temporary underperformance from evidence that the original assumptions no longer hold—and understand the cost of waiting for certainty.

Decision value: make the timing of continuation, revision, or exit explicit.

Decision intelligence

From separate analyses to one decision corridor.

AAIDIS can incorporate forecasts, scenarios, risk, external intelligence, company context, trade-offs, reversibility, and strategic alternatives into a structured decision process. The objective is not to replace executive judgment, but to give leadership a stronger basis for choosing and revising action under uncertainty.
