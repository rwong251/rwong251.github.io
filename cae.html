Business situations

See the problem—not the algorithm.

Which machines and parts should we service first?

Servicing healthy equipment wastes labor, replacement parts, and production time. Waiting too long creates breakdown and downtime risk. CAE Advanced can combine operating conditions, maintenance history, sensor readings, and visual evidence to improve the identification of assets with the greatest probability of failure.

Business value: direct maintenance spending where it is most needed while reducing unnecessary service and premature part replacement.
How much inventory should we carry?

A demand forecast alone does not determine the order. CAE can estimate demand, account for uncertainty, include service-level and working-capital constraints, determine an allocation, and test the policy under different conditions.

Business value: reduce stockouts without tying up more capital than necessary.
Which customers should receive a retention offer?

Predicting who might leave is only part of the problem. Some customers would stay without an incentive, while others may leave regardless. CAE can help identify where intervention is most likely to change the outcome and justify its cost.

Business value: protect revenue without giving away margin where the offer is unnecessary.
Should we cut price, hold, or raise it?

Pricing changes can affect volume, margin, competitor response, customer expectations, brand position, and future pricing power. CAE can test alternative pricing moves and responses before management commits.

Business value: evaluate the trade-off between demand, margin, and competitive reaction before a costly market experiment.
Did the promotion actually increase sales?

Sales may rise after a campaign even when seasonality, market growth, competitor actions, or other forces caused the change. CAE helps separate apparent success from incremental impact.

Business value: avoid scaling activities that look successful but are not creating enough additional value.
Should we expand capacity now or wait?

Capacity decisions combine uncertain demand, capital cost, operating constraints, competitor behavior, and the cost of being too early or too late. CAE can compare alternatives across plausible futures.

Business value: make capital commitments with a clearer view of upside, downside, and timing risk.
Public pricing example: PepsiCo's 2026 snack-price reversal

PepsiCo cut prices on several major snack brands by as much as 15% in an effort to revive demand. Months later, North American food volumes were still flat and the company planned partial price increases as cost and margin pressures persisted. This is the kind of decision environment a pricing war game is designed to examine before action: demand response, margin effects, competitor moves, consumer pressure, and the consequences of reversing course.
