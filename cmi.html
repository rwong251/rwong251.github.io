CMI · Market Intelligence

Understand what is changing outside the company—and why it matters inside it.

CMI brings market, economic, industry, competitive, geopolitical, climate, and other external information together so professionals can distinguish important change from noise and understand the implications for commercial and strategic decisions.

Market intelligence that leads somewhere
Signal → explanation → likely consequence → business implication → action

A market move is useful information only when the organization understands what changed and whether its decision should change too.

Market situations

The same market move can require very different responses.

Commodity prices fall 18%. Is it temporary or structural?

Inventory accumulation, new capacity, weaker demand, currency moves, weather normalization, or competitor behavior can produce similar price movements but very different strategic implications.

Business value: respond to the cause, not merely the observed price move.
A competitor announces major new capacity.

What does the expansion imply for future supply, pricing, margins, market share, and the economics of our own investment plans?

Business value: connect competitive intelligence directly to pricing, capacity, and strategic choices.
Tariffs or regulation change the economics of a market.

Which products, competitors, geographies, customer groups, and sourcing choices benefit or lose as the new rules propagate through the market?

Business value: identify where external policy change alters commercial opportunity and risk.
Weather deteriorates in a major production region.

Is the event large enough to alter expected supply, prices, inventory decisions, or customer behavior—and for how long?

Business value: convert external signals into decisions on purchasing, inventory, pricing, and market strategy.

From monitoring to interpretation

CMI is not just a feed of developments.

The product is designed to help users organize relevant market information around the business questions it affects: what changed, what may be driving it, what could happen next, which assumptions matter, and what the organization should watch as the situation evolves.

See the market change in the context of your decision.

Use CMI to connect external developments to the commercial and strategic choices they can change.
